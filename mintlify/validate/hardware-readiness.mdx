The software laboratory isolates remaining risk to the manufacturer adapter,
vendor controller, firmware, safety system, and physical environment.

## Reference fleet

- `mobile-1`: payload transport.
- `inspector-1`: destination inspection.
- `manipulator-1`: payload placement after dependencies pass.

Each emulator has an independent clock, state machine, frame, limits, battery,
health, safety state, native acceptance, cancellation, disconnection, and
recovery behavior.

## Acceptance gate

<Steps>
  <Step title="Core and protocol">Run all tests and UMP 0.1 vectors.</Step>
  <Step title="Live transports">Pass MQTT, WebSocket, OPC UA, and ROS 2 round trips.</Step>
  <Step title="Failure behavior">Pass deterministic faults, restart, and reconciliation tests.</Step>
  <Step title="Scale">Pass declared 3, 25, 100, and 250 participant profiles.</Step>
  <Step title="Visual boundary">Load Gazebo and complete the UMP-driven warehouse scenario.</Step>
  <Step title="Soak">Complete one uninterrupted eight-hour run.</Step>
  <Step title="Evidence">Generate and verify an owner-signed readiness report.</Step>
</Steps>

```bash
ump-readiness report \
  --fault-profile config/fault-baseline.json \
  --output .ump-lab/readiness.json
ump-readiness verify .ump-lab/readiness.json
```

A valid signature proves that evidence has not changed. It does not prove that
unlisted middleware, firmware, adapters, or physical equipment behave the same.
